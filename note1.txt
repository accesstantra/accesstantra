Establishing Accessible Tantra (A Non-Profit Organisation)
For Ensuring Accessibility in Each and Every Tantra — Whether Governmental or Private

1. What is Accessible Tantra?
This is the proposed name of our intended organization	 working for accessibility in each and every tantra (machinery of government). As the name suggests, the term tantra means the machinery, and hence the name implies that each and every tantra must be accessible irrespective of the government or private machinery. This will result in inclusion, productivity, and the best outcome of the differently abled.
2. Background of the Idea
The idea comes from the personal experience and experiences of our peers having any sort of disability. As it has been observed that there are various organisations that are working efficiently in the accessibility and inclusion of the differently abled, but unfortunately they are static. It means they operate in a static manner — like a pond having sweet water which only benefits the known beneficiary.
But in countries like India, where the literacy and awareness rate is so low, static operation is not very beneficial at this time. According to the 76th Round of the National Sample Survey (NSS), the literacy rate among persons with disabilities aged 7 years and above in India is just 52.2%, which is far below the national literacy rate of approximately 80% for the general population. 
Furthermore, according to Census 2011, there are 2.68 crore (26.81 million) persons with disabilities in India, constituting 2.21% of the total population. Out of these, approximately 1.50 crore are male and 1.18 crore are female, with a staggering 69.49% (1.86 crore) residing in rural areas where awareness and accessibility infrastructure remain critically inadequate.
The gender and rural-urban divide is even more alarming. In rural India, only 33% of disabled women are literate, while their urban counterparts fare slightly better at 56%. Among disabled men, the numbers are higher — 57% in rural areas and 73% in urban settings — but the gender gap remains glaring. Overall, 62% of disabled men are literate, compared to just 40% of disabled women. Additionally, only 1 in 5 persons with disabilities in India complete secondary education or higher, and nearly 3 out of every 4 individuals who were working before becoming disabled either lost their job or had to switch to a different kind of work afterward. 
Given these stark realities, the idea of Accessible Tantra emerged — an organisation that will go door to door to ensure accessibility, rather than waiting for beneficiaries to find us.
3. Work Area of This Organisation
This organisation primarily will operate in the following areas but is not limited to:
a. Digital Skills
Although there are various organisations working for the enhancement of digital skills, this will also work in this area but to fill the remaining gaps. It will cover computer and phone training to students, professionals, and old-aged persons to cater to their needs. It will also cover customised training of the students and professionals as per their needs.
b. Placements and Freelancing
It will also ensure the placement of skilled students in different industries as per the need, and support freelancing opportunities for differently abled professionals.
c. Accessibility Check
It will ensure accessibility checks in different institutions to ensure accessibility. It will also guide the concerned authority to ensure the same in their institution.
d. Accessibility-Related Training to Private or Public Offices or Organisations
It will also ensure that public or private institutions — specially educational institutes — ensure accessibility in their own institute. As it has been observed that many students purchased courses from those institutes but cannot utilise the same due to lack of accessibility.
e. Mentoring to the Students or Professionals
It is a well-established fact that there are so many students and professionals who are doing exceptionally well in their fields. Mentoring by them will ensure good results for the needy students and professionals.
f. Legal Aid for Accessibility
It will ensure legal help for accessibility for all, enabling persons with disabilities to assert their rights under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016 and other relevant legislations.
Conclusion
Accessible Tantra is not just an organisation — it is a movement. A movement to ensure that no tantra, whether governmental or private, remains inaccessible to the differently abled. In a country where 2.68 crore persons with disabilities continue to face barriers in education, employment, and daily life, and where literacy among them stands at a mere 52.2%, the need for a dynamic, door-to-door approach is not just desirable — it is imperative. Through digital empowerment, institutional accessibility audits, mentoring, placement support, and legal aid, Accessible Tantra aims to transform the static pond of existing efforts into a flowing river of inclusion that reaches every corner of India.
